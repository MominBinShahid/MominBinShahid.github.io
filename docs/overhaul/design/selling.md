# Landing pages that sell an unknown person

26 sites, all fetched and verified live 2026-09-17. Commissioned after Momin's
correction: *"it's looking like you are building a blog and you are attaching a
landing page to it. I want a full-fledged, awesome-looking landing page that sells
myself to potential clients or maybe potential employer."*

The earlier 35-site reference set was wrong for this job — those people are already
famous. An index of post titles works when you have an audience. Momin needs to
convert a stranger.

**His hard constraint: no testimonials, no client logos, no case studies, empty
blog.** Everything below is filtered through that.

**credibility that leads to a job**, mainly in Europe and the US, with consulting
second. He wants an elegant personal site that makes a stranger curious enough to

**Rejected from this file:**

  hook: *"I don't want to do this what you are doing that actually states a single

- The single repeated hard CTA. The résumé is the destination; contact is quiet.

**Still valid from this file:**
- The ten no-testimonial techniques, minus the metric-led ones.

## The structure that recurs

Across 24 fully-extracted pages:

**hero (who + outcome) → problem or what-I-do → proof (work, with numbers) →
services → process → about → writing (light) → single CTA**

| Element | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Hero with a value claim | 24/24 |
| Ends in a contact/CTA block | ~20/24 |
| Work / projects section | 14 |
| Services or offers | 10 |
| Named clients or logos | 10 |
| Writing on the homepage | 9 |
| Newsletter capture | 8 |
| About as a section, not the hero | 8 |
| **Testimonials** | **7 (29%)** |
| Explicit process / how-it-works | 4 |
| Pricing | 3 |
| Guarantee | 1 |

Testimonials are a minority. Not having them is normal, not a gap.

Consultants sell with **copy and offer structure**; engineers sell with **artifact
rosters**. The best pages for an unknown do both.

## How heroes open

| Type | Frequency | Verdict for an unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome-led offer | ~8 | Best default — sells a result, not a résumé |
| **Proof-led number** | 3 | **Strongest of all, if the number is real** |
| Visitor-problem question | 3 | Converts hard, slightly salesy register |
| Identity + specialty | ~7 | What everyone does; needs premium craft behind it |
| Warm persona | 3 | Likeable; needs proof immediately after |

**Not for Momin.** He has the numbers, and he has ruled them out as a hook — see
the scope correction at the top of this file. For him the closest usable row is
*identity + specialty*, which the table warns "needs premium craft behind it".
That is exactly the bet: the craft carries the page, the credibility comes from
category (eight years, five industries, four markets, leads a team), and the
numbers wait on the résumé.

## Ten ways to sell with no testimonials

1. **Shipped-artifact roster** with live links and one metric each. The strongest
   substitute — the work is checkable.
2. **One flagship number in the hero.** His is ready.
3. **Employer-as-proof.** *Lead Engineer at Sastaticket.pk; previously Securiti.ai,
   a unicorn; users across four continents.* Framed honestly as "built at", never
   implying they were clients.
4. **Name the visitor's exact problem.** Specificity itself signals experience.
5. **Process transparency** — a numbered how-we'd-work section. Only 4/24 do it,
   so it differentiates, and it de-risks hiring a stranger.
6. **Risk reversal** — a guarantee. 1/24 does it. Rare and potent.
7. **A stated floor** — "projects from X". Confidence signal and a filter.
8. **Public artifacts / OSS.** MealUnits is small but it is a *story* artifact —
   engineering for one person, offline, private. Memorable in a way a contribution
   graph is not.
9. **Distinctive voice as memorability.** "Code & Consciousness" is his version.
   One line of it beats six tiles of it.
10. **Corpus-as-proof** — the archive sells. **Not available to him yet.** Do not
    build the page around it.

## Where the blog goes

- Most common: **a nav link only.** Studios often omit writing entirely.
- Best pattern for thin content: **3–4 curated "featured writing" links, never a
  paginated index.**
- Consultants reframe writing as an ongoing service — "letters most Tuesdays" —
  a promise rather than an archive.
- **Nobody links an empty blog.** Writing appears on the homepage only once it
  exists.

**For Momin: launch with zero blog surface on the home page.** Add a Writing block
when the first three posts are real, and wire RSS then.

## Premium vs merely competent

The concrete separators, not adjectives:

- **One idea per viewport**, with real whitespace
- **Numbers inside headlines**, not buried in paragraphs
- **Specific named artifacts** instead of skill lists — no premium site has a
  skills grid, let alone bars
- **A single CTA, repeated** — versus five competing ones. This is the honest tell
  between selling one thing and selling everything
- **"You"-addressed copy** rather than "I"-recital
- Short declarative sentences with one earned flourish of voice
- **Restraint in motion** — animation on the hero and nothing else

## What not to do

- **Name + title hero with nothing else.** "Momin Bin Shahid — Full-Stack
  Engineer" sells nobody. This is the weakest 30% of every gallery — **and it is
  what all three of our design directions did.**
- Skill bars, percentage meters, logo-soup stack grids.
- **Six equal-weight tiles.** Flat hierarchy means no message — and it is exactly
  what buries his best material today.
- Adjectives without artifacts: "passionate", "detail-oriented", "results-driven".
- **Competing CTAs.** Download-CV plus contact plus blog plus socials all shouting
  means none chosen.
- **An empty or thin blog index on the home page.** Nothing reads "abandoned"
  faster.
- Employers dressed as a client-logo wall. Strangers check. A sentence is stronger
  and honest.
- Template recognisability — Brittany Chiang clones, Awwwards preloader clichés,
  cursor-following blobs on a page with default type. Craft signals only count when
  they are evidently his.
- Fiverr-style pricing menus. Wrong register for a lead engineer selling judgment.
  A floor, or nothing.
- **Overclaiming without a number.** "World-class engineer" loses to "an agent that
  reviews hundreds of calls a day." He already owns the second sentence; the whole
  page should be built from claims of that shape.
