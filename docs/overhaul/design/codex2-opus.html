What your team gains

01

Three things a lead engineer is actually on the hook for, and what each one looks like when I am the one doing it.

i.

Production applications get better while they stay up.

Nearly everything I have shipped was work on software that already had people on it — integrations failing at the provider boundary, a front end that took several seconds to become useful, errors nobody saw until a customer reported one. That work is diagnosis first, then a change small enough to ship without holding the product still.

ii.

Architecture decisions get made by someone who is still in the code.

At Sastaticket.pk I lead code reviews and architectural decisions, and I stay hands-on in the codebases those decisions land in. Reviewing the change and writing the change are the same job to me; a decision made away from the code is a decision made without the evidence.

iii.

Your engineers get better, and your team gets an AI capability that is not just mine.

I mentor three to five engineers day to day. When I built the AI-agent work at Sastaticket.pk I built a foundational agent structure for the team to extend, deliberately, so that every agent idea afterwards did not have to route through one person. Capability that lives in one head is not capability.

How a role conversation would work

04

No process theatre. Three steps, and it is reasonable to stop after any of them.

01

Tell me about the product and its hardest problem.

What it is, who is on it, and the thing your engineers keep circling back to. Bring the problem you would actually hand a new lead in month one — not the tidied version.

02

I walk you through shipped work that resembles it.

Which decisions I made, what I traded away to make them, and what I would do differently now. Most of my work has been closed-source, so this is me talking you through it rather than opening a demo. Ask the awkward questions here; that is the point of the step.

03

We work out what the role actually owns.

Scope, who I would be leading, how architectural decisions get made and by whom, and whether this is a fit in both directions. A lead role that nobody has defined is the most expensive hire a team can make.
