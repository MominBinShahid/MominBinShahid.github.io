01  Where this starts

You review a sample, because a sample is all a person can reach.

The material arrives faster than anyone can read it — calls, tickets, transcripts, documents. So a slice gets checked properly and the rest goes unread, and nobody can say what is in the part nobody looked at. That was the exact shape of the problem I took on.

02  Evidence

The call-QA agent, built at Sastaticket.pk

Sastaticket.pk is Pakistan’s leading OTA, processing thousands of flight bookings a day. Its support-call QA was a sampling exercise: a reviewer got through five to eight calls in a day, and the rest of the day’s calls were never heard again.

I built an agent that evaluates them at a different order of magnitude — hundreds daily, at 92% accuracy — which took 30% off the manual QA workload in its initial deployment.

It is a scheduled production system, not a demo: Airflow orchestrating Python on AWS, with Google GenAI and LangChain doing the evaluation. The agent structure underneath it was written to be extended, so the team adds new checks without rebuilding the thing.

  • Python
  • Google GenAI
  • LangChain
  • Airflow
  • AWS

92% is the measured accuracy of that system on that workload. It is a record of what was built, not a number I can promise on anyone else’s material.

Everything else — Securiti.ai, Folio3, Scayle, eight years of stack and the rest of the numbers — lives in the résumé rather than on this page.

04  Prospective

How we could work together

Nothing below is a packaged method or a timetable. It is how I would expect the work to go, and the first step is a conversation rather than a proposal.

  1. Understand the workflow, and agree what success means.

    What gets reviewed today, by whom, and what a good outcome would actually look like to the people doing it.

  2. Build it, and evaluate against representative examples.

    Your real material, not a benchmark — so the quality question gets answered on the work you care about.

  3. Integrate into the application, then plan operation and ownership.

    Where it runs, how it is monitored, and which of your engineers can extend it once I am no longer the one editing it.
