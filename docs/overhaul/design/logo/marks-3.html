One line, five ways

Every mark below is a single unbroken stroke — each can draw itself on load.

19Cursive
Cursive

Handwritten feel. Soft, warm, a signature rather than a logo.

20Angular
Angular

A zigzag with two peaks — reads as M and as a signal trace at once. Sharpest of the five.

21Looped
Looped

Two curls at the shoulders. Ornamental — best large, muddy small.

Crossed

The stroke crosses itself once. Energetic, slightly unstable — the most "drawn in one motion" of the set.

Meem line

MMIN
Meem for O

The meem replaces the first O — its bowl is already a circle. Both alphabets in one word, and it still reads MOMIN to everyone.

On the Urdu faces: Nastaliq is the traditional Urdu hand and the most beautiful, but it slopes and needs vertical room — a header or footer mark, never a favicon. Reem Kufi is the one built like a logo: geometric, upright, holds at small sizes. Changa and Cairo read as contemporary rather than traditional. Rakkas has the most personality if you want it loud.

My pick from this round: 23 Meem line — one unbroken stroke that begins as a Latin M and resolves into a meem. It animates, it is bilingual, and nobody else has it. Meem for O is the wordmark version of the same idea.
