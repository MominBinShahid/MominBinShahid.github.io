Cursor ink

Move your cursor across this box. The mark leans with you, and as you cross the midpoint the Latin gives way to the Urdu. You control the morph by hand — it is not a playback.

On the Urdu: Google Fonts carries 24 Arabic-script faces but only two real Nastaliq — Noto Nastaliq Urdu and Gulzar. Nastaliq is punishing to digitise because letters cascade diagonally and every glyph changes shape by context, so almost nobody builds one. That is a reason to draw the final mark as outlines rather than set it in a font: you stop being limited to two options and the animation stops depending on a webfont loading.

Not a GIF. All five are CSS or SVG — crisp at any size, a couple of kilobytes, theme-aware, and they respect prefers-reduced-motion (reduced motion shows both scripts at rest instead of animating). A GIF would be fixed-resolution, fixed-colour and heavier.

My picks: A4 Hover for the site header — it never loops, never nags, and rewards the one person who reaches for it. A3 Ink draw for a one-time page-load moment. A5 is the interesting one for what you asked about cursor interaction: the visitor drives the morph themselves.
