01The bottleneck

You can’t review everything. So you sample.

Support calls, tickets, chat transcripts, claims, submissions — most teams produce far more reviewable material than anyone can actually review. So QA becomes a sample: a handful of items a day, checked by hand, standing in for all the rest. The problems you most need to catch are usually in the pile nobody opened.

Sometimes the AI part already exists — a model, a prototype, a convincing demo — and the hard part is integration: your data, your permissions, your deployment, your failure modes, your existing application.

And sometimes what’s missing is a foundation your own engineers can extend.

These are the problems I want to hear about. Plainly, the work: AI-agent development, production integration, and an extensible foundation for your team. If one of these sounds like your week — Discuss your AI problem.

02EvidenceSastaticket.pk

The call-QA agent

Sastaticket.pk is Pakistan’s leading online travel agency — thousands of flight bookings a day, and a steady stream of customer-support calls behind them. Manual QA could evaluate 5–8 of those calls in a day. Everything else went unheard.

I designed and built an AI agent that evaluates those calls automatically. It now reviews hundreds of calls daily at 92% accuracy — doing in a day what took a team several, with a consistency people can’t sustain call after call.

It runs in production, inside the QA team’s actual workflow — not a demo. What it changed for that team’s workload, and the rest of the record, is in the résumé.
